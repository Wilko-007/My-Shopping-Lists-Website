CHRISTMAS PLANNING

How to Set a Christmas Gift Budget (and Actually Stick to It)

Updated 15 November 2026 · 6 minute read

Most Christmas overspending doesn't come from one big present. It comes from lots of small "that'll do" purchases nobody added up. The fix is simple: decide the total first, split it by person, and record every gift as you buy it.

1. Pick one total you can afford

Start with the number, not the list. Look at what will be left after your normal bills between now and the end of January, then decide how much of that can go on gifts. Write that figure down. Everything else in this guide is about staying under it.

Leave a little room for the extras that sneak in: wrapping paper, cards, postage for interstate family, and the work Kris Kringle you forgot about. Setting aside around 10% of the total for these is a good starting point.

2. List everyone you buy for

Write down every person, including the easy-to-forget ones: teachers, the neighbour who feeds the cat, a Secret Santa, the in-laws. Seeing the full list before you spend anything stops the budget being used up by the first few people you shop for.

3. Split the total per person

Give each person their own amount. It doesn't have to be equal, but it does have to add up to your total. Here's an example for a household setting a $600 budget:

WhoBudget
Partner$150
Two kids ($110 each)$220
Mum and Dad$80
Brother and sister-in-law$50
Teachers and Secret Santa$40
Wrapping, cards and postage$60
Total$600

Example only. Swap in your own people and amounts.

4. Record every gift as you buy it

This is the step that makes a budget work. Each time you buy something, write down who it's for and what it cost, then check what's left for that person. If one person goes over, take it from someone else's amount straight away rather than hoping it evens out.

Tip: keep receipts together in one place, or snap a photo of each one. If a gift needs returning in January, you won't be hunting for it.

5. Use the gift cards you already have

Many of us have half-used gift cards sitting in a drawer. Before you start shopping, check their balances and note them down. A $40 balance at the right store can cover a whole present and leave your budget untouched.

6. Plan with your partner without spoiling surprises

If two of you are buying presents, agree on who is buying what so you don't double up. Share the list for the kids or the grandparents, but keep your list for each other private. Christmas morning should still have a surprise in it.

7. Watch for the usual budget leaks

  • "Just in case" gifts. Spare presents for unexpected visitors add up fast. Pick one or two and include them in the budget.
  • Sales you didn't plan for. A discount only saves money if the item was already on your list.
  • Last-minute panic buying. Aim to finish most of your shopping by mid-December, when shops are calmer and delivery times are still reliable.
